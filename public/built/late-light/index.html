Old Light

Every star in this torch is the past. Sirius as it was 8.6 years ago. The Pleiades as they were in the late 1500s.

Book seats Take the trip

Thursday to Saturday, 22:00 and 23:30. 55 minutes.

RA 05h 35m · DEC −05°
Move the torch to read the chart

Under the torch the sky becomes a star chart of Orion, Taurus and Canis Major. Sirius is 8.6 light-years away, Aldebaran about 65, and the Pleiades about 440, so their light left in the late 1500s.

The journey: from the dome to the oldest light

This light left

just now

  1. 22:00 · Lights out

    Your seat in the dome

    The doors close, the room goes dark, and the projector turns the ceiling into the night above the city. Then we leave.

    Distance: zero

  2. Stop 1

    The Moon

    384,400 km away on average. The craters you can see are impact scars, some of them billions of years old.

    Light takes 1.3 seconds

  3. Stop 2

    The Sun

    Its light takes about 8 minutes 20 seconds to reach us. The Sun you see is always a little in the past.

    149.6 million km

  4. Stop 3

    Jupiter

    The Great Red Spot is a storm wider than Earth. People have watched it for more than 150 years.

    33 to 54 light-minutes, by season

  5. Stop 4

    Saturn

    Its rings are mostly water ice, and in places they are only about 10 metres thick.

    About 70 to 90 light-minutes

  6. Stop 5

    Voyager 1

    Launched in 1977, it is the farthest thing people have ever built. Its radio signals now take almost a day to reach Earth.

    Almost one light-day

  7. Stop 6

    Proxima Centauri

    The nearest star to the Sun. A small red dwarf, too faint to see without a telescope.

    4.2 light-years

  8. Stop 7

    The Orion Nebula

    A cloud where new stars are forming. On a clear night you can see it with your own eyes, a smudge in Orion's sword.

    About 1,340 light-years

  9. Stop 8

    The centre of the Milky Way

    At the middle of our galaxy sits Sagittarius A*, a black hole about four million times the mass of the Sun.

    About 26,000 light-years

  10. Stop 9

    Andromeda

    The nearest large galaxy. On a dark night it is the farthest thing most people can see without a telescope.

    2.5 million light-years

  11. Last stop

    The oldest light

    The afterglow of the early universe, set free about 380,000 years after the Big Bang. It reaches us from every direction.

    About 13.8 billion years

Then the lights come up

That was 55 minutes. Come and sit under it.
