01 — The rule

One rule. The tape rolls at 00:00 Nepal time and stops at first light over the valley. Anything recorded before midnight, however good, goes back to the artist.

00:00

The clock is the A&R.

We do not pick a sound. We pick an hour. What people play when the city has stopped asking anything of them is the only brief.

00:00 → dawn

The window is whatever the sky gives.

It closes at civil dawn, which moves through the year. Winter nights are long. June nights are short and the records are shorter.

03 — Why midnight

The city has a second voice, and it only speaks after twelve.

By midnight the generators have cut out and the last bus has climbed out of Ratna Park. What is left is the valley itself: dogs trading calls across the rooftops, a bell somewhere in Patan, a tap filling a bucket, the hum of a city that is finally not trying to be heard.

Musicians play differently inside that quiet. Slower, closer to the microphone, more willing to leave a mistake in. We built a label around that difference instead of around a genre.

So the catalogue has no sound in common. It has an hour in common.

04 — Sleeves

One sleeve for each hour of the window.

Every release is sleeved by the hour it was recorded, not by the artist. The artwork is the sky over Kathmandu at that hour, drawn in your browser, never the same twice.
