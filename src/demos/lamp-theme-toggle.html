The case for a lamp you have to reach for

Smart bulbs promised effortless light. What we lost was the small ceremony of a cord, a click, and a room that changes because you asked it to.

Ingrid Solheim29 September 20267 min read

There is a brass lamp in the corner of my study that has outlived three apartments and two marriages, and its only trick is a cord. Pull it once and the room warms; pull it again and the evening goes quiet. It does nothing on a schedule and it has never once needed a firmware update.

Designers talk about "reducing friction" as if friction were always a tax. But the cord is a promise: the light will change exactly when you act, and it will tell you so with a sound. Compare that with an app that fades the bulbs at 21:00 because a rule said so, and you'll understand why so many people keep a physical switch within reach. Read the follow-up on switches.
